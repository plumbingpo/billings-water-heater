When Your Water Heater Fails in Laurel

The urgent calls we run usually follow a pattern. On gas heaters, it's a pilot that keeps going out, a burner that lights and drops out, or a gas control valve that has failed. A little caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger mess. If the tank is leaking, turn off the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or turn the gas knob to "off" on a gas model. Clear stored items away from the heater and lay towels around the base to keep the water contained. Never ignore the smell of gas: get everyone out and call the gas utility from outside.

Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Laurel

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Laurel

In Laurel, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
