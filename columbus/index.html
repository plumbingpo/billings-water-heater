Keeping Your Columbus Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
