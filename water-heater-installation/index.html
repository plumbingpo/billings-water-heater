What makes a water heater installation different from a replacement? Usually, it is the new work. Adding a heater for an addition, relocating one out of a living space, upgrading to a larger tank or a tankless unit, or converting between gas, electric, and heat pump all require piping, venting, or wiring that did not exist before. In Billings, MT, that work also has to meet local code, which can mean permits, drain pans, expansion tanks, and seismic straps depending on the location. Billings Water Heater plans the whole job up front so the heater performs, the installation passes, and nothing gets improvised on the day.

Before any pipe is cut, we match the heater to your household. Tanks are compared by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your water needs in winter. Next, we choose the spot together. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. A heat pump model has to have breathing room, temperatures above roughly 40 degrees, and a condensate drain. Electric tankless models often require a dedicated high-amperage circuit. And wherever a leak could damage finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. Clear choices and a firm price come before any work.

When to Call for Water Heater Installation

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
