Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can spill combustion gases if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not happen again next month. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and check whether an expansion tank is missing or waterlogged. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
